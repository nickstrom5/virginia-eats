Built around how people here eat

Star ratings won't tell you which counter has fed Roanoke since 1930 or where the oysters come off the boat. Old Dominion Eats is organized the way people here eat: ham biscuits, oyster bars, classic counters, pollo a la brasa and pupusas, pho and Korean barbecue and hunt-country dining, each place checked in fall 2026 against a 2025–26 source or an official tourism listing. Apple Maps' live card supplies ratings, hours and photos.

Free, with no catch

The app costs nothing, shows no ads and sells nothing inside, and there's no account to make. The lists ship inside the app, so they open right away and keep working when the signal drops out in the mountains.

What stays on your phone

Old Dominion Eats collects nothing about you: no sign-in, no analytics, no ads. Location, if you turn it on, only puts the nearest places first, and your saved places are kept on the phone or iPad. The website has no cookies and no trackers. The privacy policy has the details.

Questions

Is Old Dominion Eats free?

Completely: no price, no ads, no in-app purchases and no account to create. It's an iPhone and iPad app on its way to the App Store, with no Android version yet; until it's out, the web search here works in any browser.

Where do the lists come from?

Three kinds of source. The ham biscuit, oyster bar, classic counter, pollo and pupusa, pho and Korean barbecue, hunt country and oldest-places lists are our own research from fall 2026, with a dated 2025 or 2026 source or the Virginia Tourism Corporation's own listing behind each place. The Fairfax Health District's permits and inspections are public records. The wider directory of 17,486 restaurants starts from open map listings (Overture Maps), filtered to the kinds that proved reliable where states publish restaurant lists.

Where can I get oysters?

Our oyster bar list has 10 hand-checked places, from the Oyster Farm at Cape Charles and Fish Hawk at the Tides Inn to Norfolk, Virginia Beach and Old Town Alexandria. In the web search, the list puts the nearest first once you share your location.

How do I check a Virginia restaurant's inspection?

Virginia's 35 health districts publish their reports on the Virginia Department of Health's inspection portal, and none posts a letter grade. The Fairfax Health District also publishes its results as open data, so Fairfax-area places in the app show their latest result, in the district's own words and dated. Every place links to VDH's portal, and we compute no grade.

Does it show which places serve alcohol?

No. Virginia ABC licenses the bars and restaurants that pour alcohol and offers a licensee list, but we couldn't read its terms of use, so the app doesn't say either way.

Does it show ratings and reviews?

Not from us. Tapping a place opens Apple Maps' own card inside the app, with Apple's current ratings, hours, photos and directions. None of our lists is sorted by anyone's rating.

Does it need my location?

No. Location only matters for putting the nearest places first. If you share it, it stays on your iPhone or iPad and never reaches us, and the app works fine without it. On this website, Near me reads your location inside your browser and nowhere else.

A place has closed, or something's wrong. How do I tell you?

Write to nick@eatsranked.com with the place's name, its town and what changed. Corrections ship with the next data update.
